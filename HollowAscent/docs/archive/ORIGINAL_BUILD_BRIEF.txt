## Goal

Build a small but polished 2D vertical climbing game inspired by the tension of games like Jump King, but with an original movement system based on parrying enemies and projectiles.

The player cannot jump normally.

Instead, they must parry incoming attacks to launch themselves upward. Every successful parry generates recoil and momentum, allowing the player to continue climbing.

If the player:

- gets hit;
- mistimes a parry;
- misses the next parry opportunity;
- or goes too long without parrying anything;

they lose momentum and begin falling, potentially losing significant progress.

The central experience should be:

> Climb by maintaining a dangerous rhythm of perfectly timed parries.

## Scope

Keep the project deliberately small and achievable.

Create:

- One complete vertical level.
- Approximately 3–5 minutes of gameplay for a successful run.
- A title screen with Play and Quit.
- A short visual tutorial.
- Several increasingly difficult parry sequences.
- Sparse recovery ledges.
- A dramatic final ascent.
- A peaceful summit and completion screen.
- Quick restart functionality.
- Completion time, number of falls, and best parry chain.
- A locally saved best time.

Do not add dialogue systems, inventories, skill trees, shops, multiplayer, procedural world generation, or unnecessary architecture.

Focus development time on the core mechanic, visual identity, responsiveness, and polish.

## Controls

Use minimal controls:

- A/D or Left/Right: limited aerial horizontal movement.
- Space or primary action button: parry.
- R: restart the run.
- Escape: pause.

The player has no conventional jump button. Successful parries are the primary source of upward movement.

## Parry movement

When the player successfully parries an attack:

- The attack is destroyed, deflected, or redirected.
- The player is launched upward by the impact.
- Horizontal input slightly influences the launch direction.
- The momentum timer is refreshed.
- The parry chain increases.

The player must use incoming attacks almost like temporary platforms, chaining them together to ascend.

Add two types of successful parry:

### Normal parry

A correctly timed parry that gives the player a reliable upward launch.

### Perfect parry

A parry performed during the center of the timing window.

A perfect parry should provide:

- a slightly stronger launch;
- a sharper sound;
- a brief crimson flash;
- stronger ink particles;
- a tiny moment of slow motion;
- and more dramatic hit-stop.

Do not make perfect parries mandatory for normal progression.

Parry spamming should not work. Include a short recovery period after each attempt while keeping the controls responsive.

## Momentum and falling

After every successful parry, the player receives temporary upward momentum.

If another attack is not parried before the momentum period expires:

- upward speed fades;
- gravity becomes stronger;
- the character begins falling;
- the current chain ends.

Communicate the remaining momentum through the character’s visuals rather than a large conventional timer. For example, an ink aura around the character could gradually fade or break apart.

Getting hit should immediately break momentum and send the player falling.

Falling should usually cost vertical progress rather than immediately displaying a game-over screen.

Include a few sparse recovery ledges throughout the level. Landing on one prevents the player from falling farther. Falling below the current recovery area returns the player to the most recent safe ledge.

Keep these recovery points limited so mistakes remain meaningful.

## Enemies and hazards

Create a small set of original hazards:

1. An ink archer that fires a clearly telegraphed straight projectile.
2. A floating paper spirit that releases a slow homing attack.
3. A masked duelist that performs a close-range sweeping strike.
4. A hanging shrine mechanism that releases falling ink blades or debris.

Every attack must have:

- a distinct anticipation pose;
- a readable sound cue;
- a clear visual telegraph;
- a consistent timing pattern;
- and an accurate visible hitbox.

The first encounters should teach each attack separately. Later sequences should combine hazards and require the player to decide which attack to parry next.

Never create unavoidable or unreadable attack combinations.

## Level structure

Build one continuous handcrafted vertical ascent:

1. A quiet starting ledge that teaches the first parry.
2. Broken cliffs with simple projectile chains.
3. A windy vertical passage requiring horizontal correction.
4. A shrine section combining several attack types.
5. A difficult final chain with several consecutive parries.
6. A silent summit that contrasts with the intensity of the climb.

The camera should show enough space above the player to understand the next threat.

Do not make the first version endlessly or randomly generated. The level should be deliberately designed and consistently testable.
